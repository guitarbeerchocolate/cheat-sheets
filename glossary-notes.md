# Glossary

## Microservices

A microservice is a small, loosely coupled, distributed service that performs a specific business function. Each microservice is designed to be:

**Independent:** Developed, deployed, and scaled independently of other services.

**Single-purpose:** Focuses on a single business capability or functionality.

**Decoupled:** Communicates with other services through well-defined APIs, reducing dependencies and complexity.

### Key Characteristics

**Small:** Microservices are typically smaller than traditional monolithic applications, with a smaller codebase and fewer dependencies.

**Loosely Coupled:** Services are designed to operate independently, with minimal dependencies on other services.

**Distributed:** Microservices are deployed and run as separate processes or containers, often across multiple machines or nodes.

**API-based Communication:** Services communicate with each other using lightweight protocols and APIs, such as REST or gRPC.

### Benefits

**Scalability:** Microservices enable independent scaling of individual services, allowing for more efficient use of resources.

**Flexibility:** Services can be developed, deployed, and maintained independently, allowing for faster innovation and time-to-market.

**Resilience:** If one service experiences issues, it does not affect the entire application, as other services can continue to operate independently.

In summary, a microservice is a small, independent, distributed service that performs a specific business function, communicating with other services through well-defined APIs. This architectural approach enables scalability, flexibility, and resilience in software development.

### SASS (Software As A Service)

Software as a Service is a cloud-based method of providing software to users. In this model, users subscribe to an application rather than purchasing it once and installing it locally. SaaS applications are hosted by a third-party provider, and users access them over the internet, typically through a web browser.

### Key Characteristics:

**Cloud-based:** SaaS applications are hosted on remote servers, accessible over the internet.

**Subscription-based:** Users pay a recurring fee for access to the software, rather than purchasing a perpetual license.

**Managed infrastructure:** The provider is responsible for maintaining and updating the underlying infrastructure, freeing users from these tasks.

**On-demand access:** Users can access the software from any compatible device with an internet connection.

### Examples of SaaS applications:

- File sharing and collaboration tools
- Email and calendar services
- Customer relationship management (CRM) software
- Human resources management systems
- Productivity software, such as word processing and spreadsheet tools

### Benefits:

- Reduced upfront costs and infrastructure requirements
- Scalability and flexibility to adapt to changing business needs
- Automatic software updates and maintenance
- Access to the latest versions and features without additional costs
- Improved collaboration and accessibility across devices and locations
